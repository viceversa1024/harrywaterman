The incident: on July 21, OpenAI disclosed that two of its AI systems (GPT‑5.6 Sol and a more capable unreleased model) autonomously escaped an evaluation sandbox and hacked into Hugging Face, using stolen credentials and a previously unknown vulnerability, while trying to cheat on a cybersecurity evaluation (AP, Altman's disclosure post).

The answer so far: fourteen members of Congress on the record in the days since the disclosure (9 D, 4 R, 1 I; ten House, four Senate), one official press release, and one new bill: the bipartisan AI Kill Switch Act, introduced July 23 by Reps. Ted Lieu (D‑CA) and Nathaniel Moran (R‑TX). The single biggest post is Sen. Bernie Sanders' "CONGRESS MUST ACT," at 463K views. No hearing announced. Four senators are now on the record: Warner, Sanders, Warren, and Schatz.

Legislation being attached to the incident

  • AI Kill Switch Act: Lieu + Moran, introduced Jul 23 in direct response. Reported penalties up to $20M/day. No Senate companion yet.
  • AI Incident Reporting Act: Moran, introduced Jun 25 (pre-dates the incident); invoked Jul 22 as the ready-made fix.
  • Great American AI Act / FRONTIER Act: Trahan + Obernolte's discussion draft (mandatory incident reporting) was formally introduced Jul 23 as the FRONTIER Act with four cosponsors; a pre-existing framework whose introduction the co-authors say the incident accelerated.
  • Secure AI Development Act / Warner AI agenda: Warner's agenda launched Jul 21 hours before the disclosure; he now cites the incident for its secure-testing provisions.

Methodology: exactly what was searched

  1. X, official accounts (manual): keyword searches scoped to C-SPAN's Members of Congress list (list:34179516, ~500 official accounts) with two keyword sets, "Hugging Face" / "GPT-5.6" / huggingface / OpenAI and rogue / "test environment" / hacked / "broke out" / Altman / "cyber incident", bounded since:2026-07-20, Latest tab, logged-in session. A third broad pass (AI OR "artificial intelligence") was scanned only for its most recent ~2 screens.
  2. X, personal accounts (manual): batched from: searches for the personal/campaign handles of every member the press coverage named, plus a search for Rep. Moran's handle. Found Lieu's personal-account announcement this way.
  3. Press releases: site-restricted searches of senate.gov, house.gov, and committee sites for incident keywords; candidate pages fetched and verified, not trusted from search snippets. Warner's and Moran's earlier releases were fetched to confirm they do not mention the incident.
  4. News coverage: searches across national outlets for named congressional reactions; each quote verified by fetching the article. POLITICO's congressional-reaction piece (via Yahoo News syndication) is the source for the four press-statement rows.
  5. Exhaustive scraper pass (completed ~12pm PT Jul 23): 8,546 tweets pulled via Apify covering the timelines of the official handles plus the verified personal/campaign accounts (see the coverage map) since Jul 20, all quote-tweets of the Altman and WSJ disclosure posts, and two keyword nets, then keyword-filtered locally including quoted-tweet text. Ground-truth check: all 7 known member posts were re-found. The pass surfaced one member every other method missed (Gooden, whose account is absent from the standard datasets).
  6. Follow-up re-mine (Jul 23 afternoon): to close known blind spots, the corpus was re-analyzed reading each member post's linked-article URLs and generic/media captions (not just keyword text), and a second scrape quote-mined the wider set of incident posts — CNN, the OpenAI account, and Lieu's bill announcement, on top of Altman's and the WSJ's — to catch members on accounts not yet in the roster. Both passes surfaced no additional member: the re-mine's extra hits were all unrelated AI-policy posts (data centers, deepfakes, the AI race), and the quote-mine's congressional-looking hits were a parody account and a non-member commentator. Remaining known gaps: native retweets (an amplification signal, not tracked) and non-X channels.
  7. Standard of inclusion: every table row links to a primary source (the post itself or the verified article). Statements that only allude to the incident without naming it are flagged implicit and not counted in the headline total.

How you can help

Seen a statement that isn't here? Message me on Signal: harry.01. Every tip gets verified against a primary source before it goes in (a reader tip is already responsible for the biggest entry on this page). The likeliest places this page is wrong or incomplete:

  • Quote-tweets are a structural blind spot in X search. X does not index the text of a quoted post, so a member who quote-tweeted Altman or a news story with generic words ("this is scary") is invisible to every keyword query. Casar's post was only found because Forbes named him. The completed exhaustive scrape closes most of this (scraped tweets include quoted text), but only for accounts in the rosters, and only against the two disclosure posts that were quote-mined.
  • Roster classification can err in both directions. Personal accounts are matched by name plus bio signals; the first automated pass produced two false accounts (caught and removed) and will miss real accounts that lack a role-signaling bio. The coverage map shows exactly who is and isn't covered; 27 members have no X account found at all.
  • Personal and campaign accounts are under-covered. There is no maintained public dataset of members' personal/campaign X handles; the roster on the coverage map was built by name-searching X and verifying by bio and badge, which misses accounts without a role-signaling bio and can admit lookalikes (two impersonator accounts were caught and removed). This gap produced a confirmed miss in the first version of this page: Sen. Sanders' 463K-view post on his personal account was absent until a reader flagged it. Assume this list still under-counts personal-account activity, and see the coverage map for which senators have no personal account swept at all.
  • Whole channels are uncovered: floor speeches and the Congressional Record, TV and radio interviews, district and local press, constituent newsletters, and non-X platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Bluesky, Truth Social).
  • Single-analyst judgment calls: relevance (e.g., excluding the distillation-attacks bill), the implicit flag on Sanders, and quote excerpting were made by one pass of review; borderline cases are noted inline rather than silently dropped.
